## Current implementation

`REGIONAL_TERRAIN_VERSION` is `regional-macro-v17-scaled-water` in [regionalTerrain.js](core/world/terrain-generator/regionalTerrain.js). [runtimeConfigs.js](wizard_game/runtimeConfigs.js) enables world placement, carving, near water, and river simulation by default.

The existing six-face 256-cell macro foundation supplies candidate traits and terrain bounds. [planetWaterPlacement.js](core/world/terrain-generator/planetWaterPlacement.js) selects spread-out canonical supporting regions. [regionalShape.js](core/world/terrain-generator/regionalShape.js) invokes the existing fuller natural/eroded height evaluator once per attempted supporting field, reads controls back, and plans a bounded basin. Accepted controls become authoritative C2 spline terrain inputs inside those fields. Ordinary fine generation substitutes the spline there and retains analytic terrain outside. This is an additional supporting-height prepass, rather than two complete fine-tile generations. Its upfront cost is substantial.

Lake planning uses spline cell hulls, a connected wet core, a dry retaining ring, and explicit erosion/micro/numerical allowances. The final 1,100 lakes have minimum certified rim clearance slightly above 0.5 m and minimum guaranteed core depth 1.5 m. These guarantees concern lake containment before outlet cuts; they do not establish attractive shorelines, fine mesh fidelity, or continuous river-bank correctness.

[regionalDepression.js](core/world/terrain-generator/regionalDepression.js) cuts the supporting controls using [regionalBasinPotential.js](core/world/terrain-generator/regionalBasinPotential.js). That potential now has seeded rotation, aspect variation, domain warping, and four spatial noise scales; it preserves interior high ground. It still operates on a supporting control grid. The terrain/water intersection supplies the visible shore.

[regionalDrainage.js](core/world/terrain-generator/regionalDrainage.js) uses a sampled A* height corridor, a curved course, actual conservative lake-depth portals, and a descending terrain-following profile. World routes use 32 m profile stations, a 4,096-node search cap, and a sampled 24 m incision budget. This is a pairwise lake connection system, with up to three incoming connections per receiver. It is not a complete planet drainage network. The 6 km centre-distance limit and bounding-radius exclusion prevent the giant lake from acquiring a river in this implementation. Rivers do not reach oceans through a coherent watershed model.

Structured records publish once through `WaterService` and `WaterGpuData`, before the first terrain tiles. All 1,100 masks and 234 river records are resident in this seed; no records are dropped. The lake lookup still has two slots per coarse cell; placement enforces that limit. Global paging is unfinished.

Near water uses separate level geometry/ribbons and terrain depth/ownership clipping; distant water uses terrain shading. `WaterRiverSim` runs a camera-following 256 m strip at roughly 1 m spacing. Its prescribed reference flow balances design pressure, mass and momentum defects while SWE disturbances evolve. It is a driven visual model, not a globally conservative hydraulic equilibrium. The initial bed query is separate bounded sampling, and simulation does not trigger a second terrain-tile generation.

## Changes in this final attempt and their return

1. **Eliminated the deliberate local lake clusters.** Production now plans one lake per supporting field instead of up to eight similar depressions. Farthest-site placement remains. In the recorded default seed, lakes with another centre within 1 km fell from 877/1,200 to 0/1,100; minimum separation became 3.07 km and median nearest-neighbour distance 5.37 km. This also reduces the count to 1,100. Natural occasional clusters are reasonable; this hard separation policy is a stopgap, not a geographic model.
2. **Added a broad scale distribution through the same basin pipeline.** Default accepted population: 812 small/natural lakes, 279 medium constructed lakes, eight large lakes, and one giant. Supporting controls are 64/128/256 per side, at canonical depths 7/6/4/3 as appropriate. Cut caps are 12 m for ordinary constructed basins, 24 m for large ones, and 80 m for giant ones. Candidate quotas of 64 large/10 giant are upper limits; only eight/one survive eligibility, containment, support, and inherited root-bound constraints in this seed. The giant is in a snowy polar area. Broader fields and larger cut caps increase initialization work; do not treat those caps as aesthetic approval.
3. **Changed the shape atlas to support mixed resolutions.** A 2,048-wide linear six-face directory, five vec4 metadata texels per region, and explicit patch offsets replace the fixed-size atlas layout. Images are packed by descending size to avoid mixed shelf-height waste. CPU tests cover row-crossing metadata at ID 409 and mixed resolutions. Native fine generation additionally checks ID 409. Uploads remain bounded, with maximum submitted batch inputs about 5.23 MB. Accepted temporary GPU images are destroyed before publication. Adapter texture-height limits are checked.
4. **Shortened and flared river mouths.** Centre-to-centre trenches are trimmed to a short overlap inside actual conservative wet coverage. Width flares continuously near each portal and bank height fades there. Segment indexing uses the actual maximum packed width so the flare remains indexed. Portal/fall arc metadata is shifted to match the shortened record. This improves the captured mouths but does not solve junction ownership, confluences, outlet morphology, or hydraulic transitions generally.
5. **Reduced shore paint.** Lake shore selection is restricted to a much smaller vertical band; river sediment falls off over approximately 1–2.5 m above water instead of 4–12 m. Shore blending is capped at 0.55. Sediment mostly uses dirt/loam, with smaller sand/rock components, and retains spatial patches and slope dependence. Broad natural sand/biome patches still occur. Some captured banks now look like grass meeting a clean cutout; texturing remains inadequate for the concept art.
6. **Added bounded lake shoreline detail.** Two derivative noise samples at 24 m and 7 m scales add at most 0.25 m to the supporting height near its water level. Existing retained micro adds at most another 0.25 m; planning reserves 0.5 m. Detail vanishes outside a ±0.5 m vertical band, before the certified dry retaining boundary, to avoid a height step where cell ownership changes. The physical noise radius currently assumes this game's 131,072 m planet; generalize that through configuration if other radii become active. The matched native enabled/disabled tile comparison is recorded below. It does not establish that this alone meets the shore quality bar.
7. **Reduced excessive foam.** Foam sources now respond to excess Froude/convergence relative to the prescribed reference, with reduced rock and disturbance gains. Rendered foam has a bounded spatial pattern instead of saturating most of the strip. Latest browser river captures have no negative/nonfinite state values; mean foam is low and the steep capture retains local white streaks. Turbulence and disturbance dynamics remain active. This is local validation, not proof for every river/window.

## Remaining problems and recommended next decisions

- **Stop treating containment/count checks as a visual bar.** Inspect both aerial and low shoreline views on live ordinary terrain, not just an accepted fixed preview. The latest temperate shore still has a broad smooth curved boundary; small lakes remain oval in some cases; grass banks are too featureless. One medium lake view also exposed coarse triangular water/terrain clipping during this attempt. Keep those rejected images in view while evaluating changes.
- **The supporting field can erase the detail the banks need.** Interpolation becomes the base terrain inside accepted regions. The giant has roughly 131 m depth-mask spacing and a coarse supporting field. A quarter-metre residual cannot recover missing metre-scale rock structure. Measure a hybrid that keeps shared coarse basin/spill data and bounded fine terrain/erosion detail against the current full-natural-height bake. A lighter macro/meso eligibility or supporting pass is allowed. Preserve a common basin/outlet representation rather than making unrelated mountain/plain generators.
- **Large lake acceptance is constrained by conservative global checks.** `regionalShape.js` compares the minimum control height against the maximum inherited root lower bound over the region. This is safe but unnecessarily restrictive when lows and highs occur at different locations. Investigate spatially local bounds before increasing cuts or relaxing guarantees. Huge regions also fail support probes frequently. More giant quotas alone will not fix the yield.
- **Use a terrain drainage skeleton with actual spill/outlet ownership.** The present nearest-lower-lake pairing is a bounded prototype. Rivers often look like excavated links. The giant cannot connect at all under current distance tests. A coarse regional drainage graph and shoreline portals should share levels/bed/coverage through tributaries, lake outlets, and oceans. Sampled profile incision checks cover stations and three lateral probes; they do not certify every bank point, especially widened mouths.
- **Diagnose render geometry and mask fidelity separately.** Near/far water, terrain mesh height, continuous shore height, conservative depth coverage, and ownership can disagree. Exact tile residency alone does not prevent clipped triangular water or coarse banks. Increasing global LOD would obscure cost and is not a justified general repair. Reuse generated height/depth where possible for near geometry; measure any extra sampling or generation explicitly.
- **Material and stone detail need more than smaller scalar blend weights.** Use slope, deposition/exposure and localized sediment masks with the existing splat pipeline. Evaluate actual tile textures and normals. The concept includes rocks/boulders and mixed bare/vegetated banks; this pass adds none. Existing Runevision erosion is already used in the supporting natural bake. Additional bank erosion must earn its compute cost against cheaper geometry/material detail; it is not automatically necessary.
- **The local simulation is deliberately driven.** Native quiet trials can fill much of the strip and infer large reference discharge even where only the narrower visible ribbon is drawn. Renderer clipping and stable reference flow do not establish realistic channel hydraulics. Diagnose sampler/reference depth and visible-channel consistency before claiming physical correctness. Do not restore unrestricted mountain SWE wholesale; prior trials drained/flooded and whitened the channel.
- **Startup and memory are unresolved.** Precompute work currently blocks initial water publication. Shader first use and loader timeout can hide a terrain-less interval. Consider early consistent coarse records, staged regional work, and bounded persistent caches with explicit startup/movement measurements. Preserve the shared terminal GPU-loss handling added previously; the initiating cause of the owner's `A valid external Instance reference no longer exists` failure is still unknown. This attempt observed no spontaneous loss and does not reproduce or explain that original cause.
