## Why the current shapes fail

The lake landform uses three overlapping smooth lobes. Their contour can be non-circular while still having very limited structure: rounded ends, a few broad bulges and little interior variation. Increasing the nominal radius does not necessarily create a large connected lake. Earlier natural-field experiments already demonstrated this failure: a large potential region can contain scattered small wet patches. The current minimum connected wet-area check is useful and should remain.

The local authoritative shape is a spline over sampled natural terrain. At this demonstration site its 256-control grid has roughly 13–14 m spacing. It cannot resolve metre-scale coves or stones. Fine terrain can provide that detail, but the lake proof currently reduces material micro across both the potential water region and the retaining ring. It uses a 0.25 m allowance divided by the global worst-case material budget of 24.4 m: about **1.02%** of the original micro amplitude. This is a source-confirmed reason for smooth close shores, independently of the broad landform shape. Exact detail also depends on local material, tile sampling and mesh resolution; a screenshot alone cannot assign all angularity to one of them.

The river problem has three coupled causes. The eight-neighbour route cost rewards short, inexpensive incision and has no explicit sustained-curvature objective. A grid path plus smoothing is a useful drainage corridor, but straight low-cost reaches remain straight. The approximately 8 m nominal channel then gets only about 1.2 m lateral wobble, with effectively constant nominal width. Finally, the two banks use an opposed shared noise signal and a broad smooth carve profile, while material micro is suppressed over the channel and bank zone. The existing river noise is seeded and nonperiodic; calling it a sine wave would be inaccurate. Its scale and amplitude nevertheless cannot turn a straight corridor into a convincing river.

Lake/river anchors also use enclosing radii rather than an actual wet shoreline portal. Those radii are especially unsuitable for an irregular lake with bays or islands. Bulb joins and extended connector trenches need a shared outlet position, water elevation and wet coverage. Additional texture, foam or shallow water simulation cannot repair those geometric choices.

## Architecture worth retaining, and architecture still on trial

The persistent six-face foundation, exact input matching and fenced ownership are useful. The foundation is a terrain-generation input with stable lifetime, rather than a cache of camera-dependent finished tiles. Sharing it across unchanged feature revisions has already demonstrated complete regeneration savings. Keep the distinction between rebuilding everything and rebuilding features over identical inputs.

The conservative spline cell hulls are also useful. They check more than selected points: an upper hull can certify a fully wet cell, and a lower hull can certify a dry retaining ring after accounting for bounded fine detail and numerical allowance. Those guarantees should constrain the final evaluator. They should not dictate a smooth artificial coast merely because that is easy to prove. A containment certificate is not a visual quality certificate.

The dense natural-height prepass remains provisional. It invokes the natural base stack, including existing erosion, then substitutes its spline in local fine generation. It is not two identical complete fine-tile generations. It does add preparation, readback, planning, interpolation and persistent data. In this increment's interleaved run, the organic local shape phase costs about 21–26 ms; the sixteen lake-area fine outputs have median original-base cost about 136.8 ms versus dry shared-shape 127.8 ms. That roughly 9 ms fine-stage difference does not by itself pay for the full shape preparation. The prepass may still earn its cost through better placement and safe connected basins. It has not earned universal use solely as a performance optimization. These outputs differ intentionally inside the depression, so they do not establish equal natural-terrain fidelity everywhere.

I recommend **one basin/outlet/channel contract with adaptive effort**, rather than separate plateau and mountain generators. The record should own a deterministic feature identity, support, water level or longitudinal profile, cut limits, fine-detail budget, connected wet component and explicit outlets. Relief, enclosure, suitable floor area and humidity can influence size, frequency and work limits. A wide mountain valley can deserve a larger lake; a tilted plateau can deserve rejection. Biome names alone are poor architectural boundaries.

Three supporting-data strategies remain credible: coarse traits and bounds only; a bounded specialized height field where confidence is insufficient; or a fuller natural-height field that fine terrain reuses. They should feed the same feature records and evaluator. The current fuller-field path supplies the controlled baseline for this experiment. This session does not claim a new measured ranking of all three. Uniform architecture does not require equal resolution or equal work at every site.

Proof strength should also be explicit. A safe wet core and retaining ring need conservative bounds. The detailed coast can move within a bounded transition band. Treating every potential-water cell as necessarily wet would erase useful high ground; treating every ambiguous cell as dry can fragment the lake. Preserve both the certified interior and the ambiguous shoreline band, with water clipped by the actual shared terrain/level crossing.

## First implemented increment

The new `organic` profile replaces the three-lobe cut weight with a compact two-dimensional basin potential. Deterministic spatial noise acts through the interior as well as the edge, so natural high ground can survive as a peninsula or small island. Its four spatial scales are tied to the 450 m landform radius; the finest is 20 m. A compact inner anchor keeps the landform from rejecting itself because its own noise removed its centre. This remains macro/bank-scale structure, **not proper fine shoreline micro**.

The potential is evaluated once on CPU while modifying regional controls. It adds no shader octave, GPU dispatch, binding or persistent texture. Controls are cut only, with the existing 8 m limit; no manufactured raised rim or new hills. The existing underwater polish remains capped at 1.5 m. The full common hull/connectivity proof still decides whether a lake exists.

Two eligibility details changed only for the organic profile. Boundary probes bracket the outermost radial support rather than assuming the noisy field has one monotone crossing. A preliminary incision check requires a feasible central anchor rather than flattening all five interior probes. Candidate ranking still uses the maximum interior probe, retaining the same selected demonstration location. The full-field proof is the final gate; eligibility probes are not guarantees. The old `lobes` branch and ordinary default remain available.

Rejected trials are preserved. The first potential trial retained the old all-interior floor requirement and rejected all nine proposals: minimum required cut 8.034 m exceeded the unchanged 8 m budget. A second trial admitted a 0.481 km² lake at a different eligibility position, which was unsuitable for a matched-location comparison. The final ranking returns to the original position `(0.3, 0.3)`. Its first proposed water level does not pass containment; the existing planner accepts a lower level, **579.576 m**, without increasing any budget.

## What the measurements establish

All complete native comparisons generate the same **23 tile addresses and requested terrain outputs**, including sixteen lake/rim/halo tiles and seven historical comparison tiles. They include shared roots, rejected placement work, regional shape and proof, quadtree selection, water publication, full terrain textures and the final completion fence. The larger lake fits that same tile coverage. Times are wall time through a GPU fence on this Apple M1 Pro host, not isolated GPU timestamps or lower-end guarantees.

| --- | ---: | ---: |
| Conservative rendered wet-area estimate | 0.187 km² | 0.527 km² |
| Estimated wet extent | 617 × 538 m | 1,090 × 936 m |
| Connected certified wet cells | 881 | 2,524 |
| Certified dry-rim margin | 0.505 m | 0.510 m |
| Complete regeneration median, first alternating comparison | 236.148 ms | 241.766 ms |
| Complete regeneration median, second alternating comparison | 241.499 ms | 244.133 ms |
| Tracked native workload peak payload | 41,575,012 B | 41,575,012 B |

The area is estimated from a conservative surface-depth texture, not an exact continuous shoreline integral; the two lake extents have different texture spacing. The increase is also evident in the same-camera actual renders and certified connected component. The paired complete-cost increase is **2.6–5.6 ms** in these small warm samples, with overlapping ranges. Report it as a modest observed quality cost, not a fixed per-tile penalty or a speedup. No persistent GPU payload increase is introduced by the potential. Report endpoints include diagnostic sampler allocations and exclude driver/pipeline storage and the much larger game pools.

The final micro-inclusive height audit reads 262,144 rendered terrain texels. Of these, 10,108 lie in cells certified fully wet; all pass the minimum 0.25 m wetness check, with observed minimum depth 1.046 m. Core and rim samples also pass. The independent continuous proof remains the spline-hull derivation, not this finite audit. The maximum observed spline interpolation error is 0.00215 m against the existing 0.125 m allowance. Sampled height comparisons show no raising or exterior changes. Candidate final-height hashes agree across independent native runs.

Actual production captures use matching world cameras and lighting: overview, oblique bank, close bank, distant view and ownership diagnostic. They show a broader connected body, a distinct bay and peninsula, and small retained high ground. Close shore remains angular and lacks the desired detail; distant terrain material/LOD boundaries are also visible. The owner subsequently accepted the broad shape as sufficient to proceed; this does not establish finished shoreline detail.

Sequential browser scene observations measured full render-to-fence medians 15.2 ms for lobes and 13.4 ms for organic, with roughly 21 ms p95. They do **not** establish a rendering improvement: residency differs across page loads, and the stored manual-diagnostic snapshot was idle rather than an inventory of resident geometry. Startup observations similarly differ with uncontrolled driver caches: native first builds roughly 1.3–2.4 s; actual game water readiness roughly 8–9 s. One native audit ran alongside CPU tests and is not an isolated cold measurement. None of these demonstrates the requested whole-view readiness below 50 ms.

Unchanged-foundation feature regeneration was measured separately after the import fix. Three alternating full/shared rounds give complete medians **245.156 / 228.225 ms**, with identical regional control hashes and generation keys. Foundation phase changes from about 14.3–14.7 ms to 0.060–0.146 ms. Whole-workload peak remains 41,575,012 B in both modes; no peak-memory saving is established for this workload. This demonstrates reuse in the existing architecture, not faster cold placement or full output equivalence from control hashes alone.

## Preview failure and repair

The owner reported `this.quadtreeGPU.setRegionalTerrain is not a function`. The served module matched disk and contained the method; HEAD's older class lacked it. A controlled browser test supplying that old module to the newer manager reproduced the exact error. Versioned imports now pair the manager with the regional quadtree API and its shader. The same stale-response test then reaches the automatic basin camera with no page/GPU errors. This supports a mixed-module explanation, but does not claim direct inspection of the owner's cache.

The first cache test incorrectly treated the already-manual startup camera as proof that the automatic basin camera had run. Its report and screenshot are retained as `cache-preview/invalid-readiness`; that is invalid preview-readiness evidence. The corrected test waits for the actual basin direction, water readiness and terrain readiness. Source import changes do not change shader contents or lake generation.

## Next increments, in order

1. **Retain the accepted broad lake.** The owner has assessed it and authorized proceeding. The current preset is one scene, not placement/yield across the planet. The measured course increment is now recorded separately; do not interpret its unfinished banks as reopening lake acceptance.
2. **Restore bounded fine shoreline detail.** Audit actual material micro magnitude/derivatives and final texture/mesh sampling at the bank. Compare a smooth bounded residual or a dedicated band-limited shoreline field against the current global-budget amplitude scaling. Keep the 0.25 m contract initially and retain the dry-ring proof. Reuse existing micro where it gives enough visible return; do not add expensive noise just because a layer is absent. Measure metres of shoreline displacement as well as height: 0.25 m on a gentle slope can move the waterline substantially. A useful result must preserve connected wet area, islands, containment and LOD consistency.
3. **Construct one convincing gentle river from its corridor.** Add sustained curvature tied to width and slope, variable width and independent left/right bank fields, with bounded curvature, self-intersection and incision. Determine centreline detail from terrain suitability within the corridor, not random per-vertex zigzags. Keep downstream water elevation monotone, while allowing a separate varying bed. Use actual wet lake portals for both ends. Evaluate close banks and the complete joined scene before extending the model to a steep reach.
4. **Apply the same contract to a rugged case.** Smaller and rarer mountain sites can reduce cost, but the common representation remains. Use localized additional supporting samples only if visual improvement earns their complete cost. Falls should follow a real terrain descent; avoid manufactured periodic steps.

Runevision erosion is already useful upstream in the natural terrain. The new depression is applied after that original erosion, so it does not receive a new bank-erosion pass. Do not rerun general erosion by default: it could add sampling cost, alter the spill/rim certificate and damage a good peninsula. Test a localized bank treatment only after bounded micro and the base course are working; re-prove altered bounds and charge the entire pass. There is no fresh measurement justifying extra erosion in this increment.

Near water geometry and Whitewater shallow water equations follow accepted lake/river placement. Both distant terrain water and future near geometry should consume the same feature levels, portals and wet-boundary/depth source. Fast-motion readiness eventually needs a separate streaming workload with visible coverage and queue latency. Queue tuning is outside this session, and the present whole-scene timings are far above 50 ms. Avoid adding mandatory fine-resolution work to first coarse visibility while designing the next feature contract.
