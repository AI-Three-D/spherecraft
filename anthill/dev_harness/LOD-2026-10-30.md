Edited regions are divided into 16-cell patches: 4 m per side with the fixture's 0.25 m voxels. An ES module worker extracts the full 3D surface using a consistent tetrahedral decomposition, then contracts connected interior vertices into progressively simpler meshes. Vertices on patch faces stay pinned, including their face diagonals, so neighbouring patches share the same border at every LOD. Different LODs do not need transition skirts or a coordinated neighbour swap. Exactly planar contractions can remove triangles without changing the surface: the flat-patch test reduces 2,048 triangles to 224, an 89% reduction.

The view selects geometry with an estimated 0.2-pixel error target. Orthographic selection follows pixel footprint; perspective selection also uses conservative distance to each patch. Interpolation uncertainty and accumulated contraction displacement contribute to the estimate. Ambiguous and close surfaces retain density rendering. These estimates are not a certified global Hausdorff bound for every possible edited field; the image checks below describe the visual evidence for the tested scenes.

Within a mesh, vertices morph continuously onto the next level before degenerate triangles disappear. Reversing zoom follows the same morph in reverse. Foldover checks cover the whole morph interval, including intermediate positions. Completed patches reveal over 350 ms using a stable per-pixel pattern. Settled geometry does not animate that pattern. A raster pass supplies candidate hit positions and the distance back to their patch entry. Camera rays check preceding density before accepting the candidate; a new obstruction in another patch cannot be skipped. Artificial cut faces still intersect the exact density field.

Editing changes density immediately and revokes the affected mesh patches, including both sides of a changed border sample. A patch waits for 250 ms of quiet before rebuilding. Snapshots copy a few rows at a time, check a local generation during copying, and check it again when the worker returns. Edits to that patch reject stale results; unrelated digs preserve useful work. Only one worker job is outstanding. GPU uploads use segments of at most 32 KiB and at most 128 KiB per update. Indexing, snapshots, and uploads share a 1 ms cooperative main-thread target. Resident geometry has a 64 MiB budget; eviction restores density rendering.

The continuation also removed the dense mesh coverage grid and the initial whole-mine sort. Existing chunk tables are scanned in small segments. Continuous camera movement no longer restarts the pending-patch scan before later patches can be visited. Settled patch uniforms are reused. The edit/render race fix removes a released patch from the current frame's draw list as well as from residency, preventing use of destroyed GPU buffers after a synchronous edit hook.
