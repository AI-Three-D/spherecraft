# Regional water flow: common placement, supporting heights and first-tile publication

2026-10-10, through increment 32. The course/height comparison below is increment 30; the bank/material update at the end records the latest owner feedback and cleanup. The owner accepted increment 29's broad organic lake shape and authorized proceeding. Its shoreline still needs polishing; do not reopen broad lake topology without evidence. The owner also clarified why the earlier two-stage design failed: generating ordinary render tiles and then regenerating them with river data caused visible delays of one or two seconds. **Water placement must precede the first terrain outputs.** A bounded supporting-height computation is allowed when its measured return justifies it; repeating complete terrain tiles is not an acceptable placement workflow.

## Decision

Keep one basin/channel representation and vary the supporting data when terrain demands it. Do not create separate mountain and plateau placement systems yet. The mountain comparison demonstrates that omitting terrain relief can violate incision limits, while the existing natural-height evaluator supplies usable guidance through the same course model. It does not establish that every mountain needs a dense field, or that plains can universally use coarse heights alone.

Keep the natural terrain-aware route sampler as the default. The new foundation-only sampler is opt-in. On the gentle case, its combined grid/profile sampling median is 1.123 ms versus 1.535 ms for natural heights. Complete generation medians differ by only 0.811 ms, with overlapping ranges and changing signs in paired rounds. On the mountain case, foundation guidance rejects the profile at 41.098 m of required cut against the unchanged 24 m budget. There is insufficient return to adopt it globally.

The new course is useful progress from a straight corridor: it bends over sustained distances, varies nominal width and joins the accepted lake at its measured wet edge. **Its close banks remain too smooth.** This increment does not meet the river reference quality, finish bank micro, establish world-wide placement or demonstrate a coarse view within 50 ms. Rendering and shallow-water simulation remain subsequent work.

## Why descending coarse regions are useful but insufficient

Use coarse regional elevations to select drainage direction and receiver candidates. They provide a reusable network skeleton and can eliminate hopeless searches before local terrain work. A region-to-region edge should carry a shared boundary crossing and receiver water level. A fine course then stays in that corridor and fits the actual landform.

Simply choosing a lower neighbouring region does not solve flat regions, local sinks, saddles, cross-face boundaries or confluences. A sink must terminate in a lake or use an explicitly planned spill connection; it cannot be silently connected through a high ridge. A flat needs deterministic tie-breaking. Region ownership and shared crossing records must make the network independent of camera position and generation order. Spill routing can use bounded floods over coarse supporting data; the expensive historical full-height graph is not a reason to prohibit every coarse flood.

Keep terrain height, bed height and water surface separate. A river can descend between two lower regional water levels while still requiring an unacceptable ridge cut. Conversely, a useful course can follow a valley and contain a rapid or fall; linearly interpolating endpoint levels can require deep artificial trenches. Coarse height is a placement proxy, not a bound on the final channel. Enforce downstream surface monotonicity, local incision and lake-level continuity after corridor selection.

The current code already selects a lower certified receiver and searches a bounded eight-neighbour height grid. This increment refines that **one local connection**; it does not introduce a global drainage DAG, rainfall accumulation, confluence scheduling, cross-face course ownership or feature paging. Those remain explicit architecture work after the local bank model becomes convincing. Do not describe the existing A* search as a newly implemented world flow network.

## Generation order and reuse

The intended order is persistent terrain inputs → basin/receiver records → bounded course/profile supporting samples → water/carve publication → terrain outputs. Render tiles do not discover the river. Coarse visibility and high-resolution descendants use the same published feature version.

The existing local natural-height prepass includes Runevision erosion and is reused as the authoritative base spline inside its support. Fine terrain does not run that same natural base again there. It still has preparation, readback, proof and interpolation costs, and its guard can fall back to the natural evaluator. The prepass has not earned universal use merely as a speed optimization; the previous lake comparison established a useful visual return at one site.

For the gentle river, the new sampler reads macro foundation inputs plus any existing authoritative local shape. It samples 1,098 coarse sites and 1,197 profile sites. Natural guidance samples the same 1,098-site grid and 1,215 profile sites on its slightly different course. These are bounded placement samples, not material/normal/splat/render tile generation. They do not produce ordinary tiles which are later replaced. Natural guidance also reuses the authoritative shape where available; outside it, it evaluates the existing natural base. Both modes subsequently generate each requested terrain output with water already bound.

The foundation sampler includes continental shelf, regional elevation, inland uplift, ocean blending and soft clamping; it deliberately omits natural relief, meso and erosion details outside the shared shape. Existing macro seam fallback remains possible. The compiled supporting shader bundle still contains the certificate/natural functions; this experiment changes the sampled entry point, not every compilation cost.

A future adaptive policy should use observed/bounded relief, slope, enclosure, usable basin area and incision confidence, rather than a biome label. Escalate only uncertain candidates. Reuse supporting data in fine terrain when appropriate. The present two-case comparison supports that direction but does not calibrate a safe automatic threshold. No automatic mountain fallback or global backend selector was added.

## Implemented course and lake connection

`regionalRiverCourse.js` refines the existing coarse route before render tiles. Its lateral search uses bilinear coarse heights, deterministic smooth noise and an excavation cost. Maximum requested offset is bounded by coarse spacing and channel width; descent reduces bend amplitude. End envelopes preserve the lake anchors. A final smoothing pass avoids per-vertex zigzags. Proper segment crossings are rejected, and the existing point budget is checked before the lateral search and intersection scan. The crossing check is not a proof of bank clearance or rejection of every touching/collinear reach.

The gentle natural-height case requests at most 36 m of lateral offset at a 256 m wavelength. Its 405-point course is 6,404.273 m long. Nominal half-width varies 10.455–13.802 m instead of a constant 12 m. Width is baked once into existing records, with conservative indexing expanded for its maximum; no new fine-terrain noise octave, shader binding or per-tile supporting pass is added. Existing GPU river shaping still applies. The light-height course is different: 399 points and 6,309.474 m. Equal coverage in the cost comparison does not mean identical terrain output bytes between these designs.

`regionalLakePortal.js` follows the course through the already available conservative lake-depth mask and records its first contiguous wet exit. The natural gentle source portal is 527.165 m along the course, versus the lake's 617.556 m enclosing radius; the light route exits at 399.693 m. Portal uncertainty includes mask spacing (9.649 m) and course sampling. This avoids a new terrain-height solve and a false circular shore assumption. The receiver in this gentle case is still an older analytic 100 m basin with a radius anchor; replacing that remaining approximation requires an actual receiver wet-boundary representation. Both ends of the mountain case have depth-mask portals.

The natural mountain course uses the same formulas, reducing requested lateral offset to 1.645 m. It is 859.371 m long with a 434.548 m descent and two localized approximately 4.93 m falls. These are terrain-following profile results, not a new waterfall renderer. The common curve model does not imply identical appearance, scale, resolution or supporting work in every landform.

## First-tile publication defect found and repaired

Source inspection showed that feature records were ready during generator initialization, but the game initially constructed GPU water buffers on its first update and filled them only on the following update. A production-page probe confirmed four coarse root tile calls with water not ready, followed by a river invalidation. Binding empty buffers was not sufficient. This was a remaining route to dry-first generation, even though local route placement itself preceded tiles.

`GameEngine` now initializes and uploads structured water immediately after the generator's regional preparation and before quadtree initialization. It clears the initial changed-cell list only while no tile streamer exists: initial inputs cannot invalidate nonexistent outputs. Genuine later changes retain their existing invalidation behaviour. The upload uses ordinary queue ordering; no second terrain generation, extra supporting-height pass, queue rewrite or completion-fence wait was introduced.

The gentle production probe before the repair observed 828 tile calls, four not ready, and one river invalidation. The corrected run observed 542 calls, **zero not ready, zero unbound and zero river invalidations**. The mountain run observed 633 calls with the same zero counts. Tile counts differ with ordinary residency/scheduling and are not a measured speedup. All runs have zero page/GPU errors; an unrelated favicon 404 is recorded. These are finite startup checks, not a fast-motion coverage guarantee or a claim that ordinary geometry/material refinement never repeats work.

The initial after-repair capture helper could accept a missing initial-load state as ready. Its first-tile observations remain valid, but its early mountain screenshot contains incomplete residency and is not a complete terrain-readiness check. The corrected mountain probe waits an actual completed state and observes 979 tile calls, all three failure counts zero. Call-start readiness does not establish exactly when each old GPU command consumed empty/filled buffers; the repair establishes publication before work begins.

## Increment 34: dense population without duplicating terrain passes

The owner rejected increment33's exploration density (61 total lakes/13 rivers). The old cap of48 source regions and ~12km source-region spacing were deployment constraints rather than a justified density target. Raising the cap with independent full256-cell lake fields would multiply startup work and memory. The implemented compromise shares one128-cell eroded supporting field and prepared continuous cell hulls between up to8 lakes. Gentle terrain proposes bounded organic depressions at jittered, disjoint supports with a larger central proposal; rough terrain enumerates natural minima. Every accepted basin retains the same wet-cell/dry-ring proof. This is one placement mechanism with terrain-sensitive candidate proposals, rather than separate mountain/plateau water pipelines.

A geometry-only incremental farthest-site order spreads accepted fields over land. Rivers route to existing lower certified lakes rather than searching for a separate analytic receiver for every source. Routes retain the natural supporting-height guidance, organic course/portals and24m incision limit. Each source has at most one outgoing connection and a receiver at most three inputs; levels descend strictly. Network crossings, ocean outlets and discharge-conserving junctions are not solved here.

The existing two-slot lake lookup stays bounded. Crowded potential footprints are rejected before publication instead of silently disappearing; all1200 final lake cores, masks and320 river records pass publication checks.
