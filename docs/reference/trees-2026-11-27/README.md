# Trees: references for the tree task (2026-11-27)

The owner, 2026-11-27, while the grass was being priced: "The reason why grass would need to be around 2 ms is that we
need trees with this detail and quality. I would expect it to eat closer to 10 ms if not more."

So the budget the trees are planned against: about 10 ms or more of the frame, with grass held near 2 ms.

The five pictures came inline in the conversation, not as files, and could not be copied here. Drop them into this folder
(any names). What they show:

1. **Open pine woodland, ground level.** Tall, thin-trunked pines with sparse, airy crowns. A stony dirt path through
   bright green ground cover, with patches of grass and moss. Soft haze and backlight through the canopy, a mountain behind.
2. **Spruce forest over a winding stream.** Dense, layered spruces and firs, some with rust-brown dead branches; low sun
   through haze. Red-brown sandy banks, dark boulders, clumps of grass and white flowers along the water.
3. **Meadow slope between pine stands.** Tall straight trunks; small young spruces dusted with snow among the rocks; strong
   cast shadows of the trees across a tall-grass meadow with seed heads and small white flowers.
4. **Redwood forest** ("Procedural Biome: Redwood Forest"). Huge, deeply furrowed red-brown trunks with moss; a shaded floor
   of needles and dirt; understory saplings and ferns; haze between distant trunks.
5. **Aerial view of a pine forest on a ridge.** A dense conifer stand along a crest; a dirt road along the forest edge;
   golden dry grassland and heath on the slopes around it; strong aerial perspective toward the sea.

Common threads: conifers first (pine, spruce, fir, redwood); individual trees readable at middle distance; trees casting
shadows onto the grass and ground (see also the grass note on shading grass under trees); haze and light shafts between
trunks; a varied floor (needles, dirt, moss, stones, grass clumps) under and between the trees.
